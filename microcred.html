Foundations of AI Microcredential

Designing a scalable, asynchronous Canvas microcredential that helps educators build a shared foundation for understanding, using, evaluating, and adapting to generative AI in educational settings.

Role: Instructional Designer & AI Learning Experience Collaborator Tools: Canvas, Articulate, generative AI tools, assessment design, scenario-based learning

Project Overview

I contributed to the design and development of the Foundations of AI Microcredential, a cross-sector learning experience for PreK-12, CareerTech, higher education, and library professionals. The course was designed as a shared entry point for educators who need practical AI literacy before moving into more specialized, sector-specific training.


The microcredential emphasizes generative AI as an arrival technology rather than a single tool. Learners build common vocabulary, examine how large language models work, practice structured AI use, evaluate outputs for accuracy and bias, and consider policy, privacy, safety, and institutional alignment. The design balances conceptual understanding with immediately applicable artifacts, reflective practice, and auto-graded checks that allow the course to scale without requiring ongoing facilitator support.

The Challenge

Educators across sectors are encountering AI at different levels of readiness. Some are experimenting heavily, while others are unsure how to begin, what risks to consider, or how to talk about AI with colleagues, students, and families. This created a need for a foundational learning experience that could establish shared language, support responsible experimentation, and prepare participants for more advanced professional learning.


The course also needed to be sustainable. Because AI tools change quickly, the instructional design could not be tied to one product or workflow. We needed a Canvas-based structure that was tool-agnostic, accessible, and easy to update, while still providing hands-on practice, clear guardrails, and meaningful data from the pilot.

Design & Approach

  • Helped shape a modular Canvas learning experience organized around AI literacy, responsible use, ethics, policy, and synthesis.
  • Supported a cross-sector design model so examples and scenarios would feel relevant to PreK-12, CareerTech, higher education, and library participants.
  • Applied the four-part AI literacy framework of Understand, Use, Evaluate, and Adapt to connect vocabulary, practice, critique, and long-term professional growth.
  • Designed learning activities that combine direct instruction, applied practice, and critical reflection so participants do more than simply consume AI content.
  • Created scenario-based assessment ideas for evaluating AI outputs, identifying privacy risks, analyzing policy decisions, and selecting appropriate responses to ethical dilemmas.
  • Prioritized auto-graded and completion-based assessments to support scale, including pre-assessments, test-out pathways, concept checks, prompt comparison quizzes, and post-assessment synthesis.
  • Contributed to experimentation-space planning, emphasizing free or accessible tools, structured prompts, sector-specific scenarios, and reflection points for pilot data collection.
  • Helped foreground responsible practice, including human-in-the-loop decision-making, data privacy, bias recognition, academic integrity, and transparent AI use.

Key Learning Design Features

  • Start where they are: Diagnostic pre-assessments allow experienced participants to test out while giving others a clear snapshot of what they need to learn.
  • Tool-agnostic practice: The course teaches transferable concepts and frameworks instead of depending on a single AI platform that may change or disappear.
  • Immediately applicable outcomes: Each module ends with a decision, artifact, mental model, or reflection participants can use in their educational context.
  • Ethics embedded throughout: Privacy, bias, safety, academic integrity, and institutional policy are treated as core design concerns rather than add-ons.
  • Built for iteration: Pilot reflections, assessment data, and learner feedback are positioned as inputs for improving both the foundation course and future sector-specific microcredentials.

Outcomes & Impact

  • Contributed to a complete microcredential blueprint with module structure, learning objectives, activities, and assessment strategy.
  • Helped align course design around scalable Canvas delivery, including prerequisites, mastery/test-out considerations, and auto-graded assessments.
  • Supported the development of a shared AI literacy foundation that can prepare educators for K-12, CareerTech, higher education, and library-specific pathways.
  • Strengthened the course emphasis on educator judgment, transparency, and responsible AI partnership rather than tool-centered adoption.
  • Established design patterns for future microcredentials, including cross-sector scenarios, structured experimentation, and ongoing data-informed improvement.
  • Within one week of rollout, the microcredential had 208 learners enrolled.
